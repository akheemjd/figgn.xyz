How to Use the TDEE Calculator

  1. Enter weight, height, age and sex.
  2. Pick an activity level — most people overestimate by one step, so choose the lower when unsure.
  3. Maintenance is roughly what you burn per day at that activity.
  4. Gradual loss and gain are ±500 calories, floored so the target never drops below your BMR.

How It's Calculated

TDEE is your resting burn multiplied by how much you move.

BMR = Mifflin-St Jeor (weight, height, age, sex)
TDEE = BMR × activity multiplier

Worked example: a 30-year-old man, 82 kg and 178 cm, has a BMR of 1,788. At a moderate activity multiplier of 1.55 that is 2,771 calories a day at maintenance, and 2,271 for gradual loss.

The deficit is floored at your BMR. Eating below resting requirements for extended periods costs lean mass, tanks energy and rarely holds. If a 500-calorie deficit would take you under BMR, this calculator gives you BMR instead and says so.

Treat any figure here as a starting hypothesis. The honest method is to eat at the estimate for two or three weeks, track weight trend rather than daily readings, and adjust by 100 to 200 calories from what actually happens. Your body is the measurement; this is the guess.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is a TDEE estimate?

Within roughly 10 to 15% for most people, which on a 2,500-calorie day is a few hundred calories either way. The activity multiplier is the largest source of error, because it is a self-assessment rather than a measurement. Use the estimate as a starting point and let two or three weeks of weight trend correct it.

What if my weight stops changing?

Plateaus are normal and usually mean your expenditure has drifted down with your weight, or that intake has crept up. Recalculate at your new weight first, since a lighter body burns less. If that does not explain it, tighten tracking for a week before cutting calories further — measurement drift is a more common cause than metabolism.

Should I eat below my BMR?

Not as a routine approach. Sustained intake below resting requirements is associated with lean mass loss, low energy, poor training performance and high dropout rates. If your goal seems to require it, the better lever is usually adding activity rather than removing more food — and if the gap is large, that is a conversation for a dietitian or doctor.
