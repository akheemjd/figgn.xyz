How to Use the Fence Calculator

  1. Enter the total fence length in feet, and the number of gate openings.
  2. Set post spacing — 8 ft is standard, 6 ft is stiffer for windy sites or slopes.
  3. Choose the height and picket width. 6 ft with 1×6 pickets is the common combination.
  4. Read the counts. Posts include one at each end, and concrete assumes two 60 lb bags per hole.

How It's Calculated

A fence is a series of sections between posts, so the post count is always one more than the number of sections — the fencepost problem, literally.

Sections = ⌈ length ÷ spacing ⌉
Posts = sections + 1 + (2 per gate)
Pickets = ⌈ (length × 12) ÷ picket width ⌉

Worked example: 100 ft at 8 ft spacing is 13 sections, so 14 posts. A 6 ft fence takes 3 rails per section — 39 rails at 8 ft each. Butted 1×6 pickets at 5.5 inches wide need 219 pickets, and 14 holes take 28 bags of 60 lb concrete.

Rails follow height: two for a 4 ft fence, three for 6 ft and taller. Pickets here are butted edge to edge for a privacy fence — for a spaced picket fence, add your gap to the picket width before dividing.

Post holes should be a third of the fence height deep, or below your local frost line, whichever is deeper. A 6 ft fence wants a 2 ft hole at minimum. Each 10-inch-wide hole at that depth takes roughly two 60 lb bags of concrete once the post itself displaces some volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far apart should fence posts be?

Eight feet on centre is standard for wood fences and matches stock rail lengths. Six feet is stiffer and worth it in high wind, on slopes, or with heavy panels. Chain link commonly goes to 10 feet. Whatever you choose, keep it consistent — uneven bays are the thing people notice from the road.

How deep should fence posts be set?

A third of the above-ground height, or below the frost line, whichever is deeper. A 6 ft fence means at least 2 feet down. In cold climates the frost line governs: a post set above it will heave and lift the whole run out of level over a couple of winters.

How much concrete per fence post?

About two 60 lb bags per post for a typical 10-inch-wide hole 2 feet deep. Wider or deeper holes take more — a 12-inch hole 3 feet deep is closer to four bags. Corner and gate posts deserve bigger holes and more concrete, since they carry the load of the whole run.
