How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your stats: Weight, height, age, and sex.
  2. Choose activity level: Sedentary (desk job), Light (1-2 days/week), Moderate (3-5 days), Active (6-7 days), Very Active (physical job + training).
  3. Select your goal: Lose (500 cal deficit), Maintain, or Gain (500 cal surplus).
  4. Your daily calorie target and macro breakdown appear instantly.

How It's Calculated

BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor):

Male: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
Female: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier

Goal: Lose = TDEE − 500 cal | Maintain = TDEE | Gain = TDEE + 500 cal

Macros (30/40/30): Protein 4 cal/g, Carbs 4 cal/g, Fat 9 cal/g

FAQ

Is 30/40/30 the right macro split for me?

It's a solid starting point. Protein at 30% (about 1.6–2.2 g/kg for most people) supports muscle maintenance. Adjust up if you're strength training heavily, or down if you prefer higher carbs for endurance sports. Listen to your body and tweak over time.

How big should my calorie deficit be?

This calculator uses a 500-calorie deficit for weight loss, which yields about 1 lb/week. This is a safe, sustainable rate. Larger deficits can cause muscle loss and metabolic adaptation — stick with moderate deficits for long-term success.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

Your activity multiplier already accounts for exercise. If you selected "Active" or "Very Active," those calories are baked into your TDEE. Only add extra calories for unusually long or intense sessions beyond your normal routine.

What if my goal calories are below BMR?

This shouldn't happen with moderate activity levels. If it does, your activity level may be set too low or your goal deficit too aggressive. Eating below BMR for extended periods can slow metabolism — consider increasing activity instead of cutting calories further.
