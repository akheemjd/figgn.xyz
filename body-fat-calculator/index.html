The Navy Method uses circumference measurements and is considered one of the most accurate at-home estimation methods. Results are estimates — DEXA scan is the gold standard.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Choose a method: Navy (tape measure) or BMI-based estimate.
  2. Navy Method: Measure neck at Adam's apple, waist at navel, and (for females) hips at widest point. Use inches.
  3. BMI Method: Enter height, weight, age, and sex for a quick estimate.
  4. Results update instantly — your body fat %, lean mass, fat mass, and fitness category appear automatically.

How It's Calculated

Navy Method (Male):

BF% = 86.010 × log₁₀(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log₁₀(height) + 36.76

Navy Method (Female):

BF% = 163.205 × log₁₀(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log₁₀(height) − 78.387

BMI Method:

BF% = (1.20 × BMI) + (0.23 × age) − (10.8 × sex) − 5.4
sex = 1 for male, 0 for female

FAQ

How accurate is the Navy Method?

The Navy Method is typically within 3–4% of DEXA scan results when measurements are taken correctly. Accuracy depends on consistent measuring technique — use a flexible tape measure and measure at the correct anatomical landmarks.

What's the difference between Navy and BMI methods?

The Navy Method uses body circumference measurements (neck, waist, hips) and is more accurate for athletic individuals. The BMI-based method is a rough estimate using height, weight, and age — it's less accurate for muscular people but provides a quick ballpark.

What is lean body mass?

Lean body mass is everything in your body except fat: muscle, bone, organs, water, and connective tissue. It's calculated as your total weight minus your fat mass.

What's a healthy body fat percentage?

For men, 14–17% is considered fit; for women, 21–24%. Athletes typically carry 6–13% (men) or 14–20% (women). Essential fat — the minimum for basic health — is 2–5% in men and 10–13% in women.
