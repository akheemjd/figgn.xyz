How to Use the Body Fat Calculator

  1. Choose a method — the Navy tape method is much better than the BMI estimate if you have a tape measure.
  2. Measure neck below the larynx and waist at the navel; women also measure hips at the widest point.
  3. Enter height, weight and sex.
  4. Read the percentage, category, and the split between lean mass and fat mass.

How It's Calculated

The US Navy circumference method uses the relationship between girth measurements and body density. It needs different measurements for men and women because fat distribution differs.

Male:   86.010 × log₁₀(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log₁₀(height) + 36.76
Female: 163.205 × log₁₀(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log₁₀(height) − 78.387
all measurements in inches

Worked example: a man 70 inches tall with a 34-inch waist and 15-inch neck: 86.010 × log₁₀(19) − 70.041 × log₁₀(70) + 36.76 ≈ 17.5%.

Lean mass is total weight minus fat mass, and it is often the more useful number of the two — during a diet, holding lean mass steady while fat mass falls is exactly what you want to see.

The Navy method typically lands within 3 to 4 percentage points of a DEXA scan, which is respectable for a tape measure. The BMI-based estimate is considerably rougher and should be treated as a ballpark only.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which body fat method is most accurate?

DEXA scanning is the practical gold standard, followed by hydrostatic weighing and air displacement. Of the at-home options, the Navy tape method is the most reliable and typically lands within 3 to 4 percentage points. Bioimpedance scales are heavily affected by hydration and can swing several points day to day.

How accurate is the Navy formula?

Reasonably, for a method that costs the price of a tape measure. It was validated on military populations and performs less well at the extremes — very lean and very heavy individuals both tend to be estimated poorly. Its real strength is consistency: measured the same way each time, it tracks change well even if the absolute number is a little off.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

Broadly 14 to 24% for men and 21 to 31% for women, with athletes below those ranges. Lower is not automatically better — fat is required for hormone production, temperature regulation and organ protection, and very low levels carry real consequences, particularly for women. Context and individual health matter more than hitting a number.
