How Much Gravel Do I Need for a Driveway?

3 August 2026

Gravel driveways look great and cost a fraction of asphalt or concrete. But ordering the wrong amount — too little and the truck makes a second trip, too much and you have a pile of rock sitting in your yard for years. Here is how to get it right the first time.

1. Measure the driveway

Length × width gives you the area. For a typical two-car driveway that is about 20 feet wide and 40 feet long, that is 800 square feet. If the driveway flares at the street or widens at a turnaround, break it into rectangles and add them together. Circle drives are π × radius², measured to the centre line of the path.

2. Pick a depth

  • New driveway: 4–6 inches. The deeper end is for heavy vehicles or soft subgrade. Most residential driveways are fine at 4 inches after compaction.
  • Top dressing: 2–3 inches. Enough to refresh the surface and fill low spots without building the whole profile back up.
  • Path or light-use area: 2 inches.

3. Convert to cubic yards

Gravel is sold by the cubic yard in the US. One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. The formula:

Area (sq ft) × depth (inches) ÷ 12 ÷ 27 = cubic yards

An 800 sq ft driveway at 4 inches is 800 × 4 ÷ 12 ÷ 27 = 9.9 cubic yards of finished gravel.

4. Account for compaction

Gravel compacts. Depending on the stone, you lose 10–15% of volume once it is rolled or driven on. Multiply your total by 1.15 to cover it: 9.9 × 1.15 = 11.4 cubic yards ordered. Round up — gravel is cheap, a second delivery is not.

5. Tons vs yards

Some suppliers price by the ton, some by the yard. The conversion depends on the stone:

  • Crushed stone or road base: 1 cubic yard ≈ 1.4 tons
  • Pea gravel: 1 cubic yard ≈ 1.3 tons
  • River rock: 1 cubic yard ≈ 1.2 tons

So 11.4 cubic yards of crushed stone ≈ 16 tons. Confirm the supplier's density number before placing the order.

6. Prep before the truck arrives

  • Excavate to the right depth so the finished gravel sits at grade.
  • Lay landscape fabric underneath to stop the stone migrating into the soil.
  • Have a rake, shovel, and wheelbarrow ready. A load of gravel spread by hand is a workout.
