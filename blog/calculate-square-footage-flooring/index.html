How to Calculate Square Footage for Flooring

3 August 2026 · 4 min read

Flooring is sold by the box, priced by the square foot, and manufactured in batches that do not quite match each other. That combination means one thing: work out the whole job before you order any of it.

Measure the room

Length times width, in feet, measured at the widest points. Measure at floor level rather than at waist height — walls are rarely parallel, and the floor is what you are covering.

Measure into doorways up to the point where the flooring will stop, usually the centre of the door when closed. Those thresholds add up across a house.

Irregular rooms

Break the space into rectangles. Sketch the floor plan, split it into rectangular sections, measure each one, calculate its area, then add the results together. Closets, alcoves, bay windows and hallway extensions each count as their own rectangle.

For a diagonal or angled section, treat it as a triangle: half the base times the height. Curves get measured as the rectangle that contains them, which over-orders slightly — that is the correct direction to be wrong.

Add a waste factor

Waste covers offcuts at the walls, damaged or defective planks, mis-cuts, and the pieces you keep back for future repairs.

  • 10% — a simple rectangular room, straight-lay installation.
  • 15% — tile, or a room with lots of doorways and obstacles.
  • 20% — diagonal or herringbone layouts, heavily irregular rooms.

Diagonal patterns waste more because every board meets the wall at an angle and the offcut is rarely reusable.

Convert to boxes

Divide your total including waste by the square feet per box printed on the packaging, then round up. Box coverage varies: hardwood is often 20 to 25 sq ft, LVP 20 to 30, tile 10 to 15. Two products at the same price per square foot can differ substantially per box.

12 × 14 ft room, 10% waste, 22 sq ft boxes

Area: 12 × 14 = 168 sq ft

With waste: 168 × 1.10 = 185 sq ft

Boxes: 185 ÷ 22 = 8.4

Materials behave differently

Tile needs 15% minimum for breakage as well as cuts, and more for large format, where a single bad cut wastes a big piece.

Carpet is sold by the square yard in some markets — divide square feet by 9. It also comes in 12 ft wide rolls, so room width drives waste more than area does. A 13 ft wide room wastes a great deal of a 12 ft roll.

Vinyl plank and laminate are the most forgiving. Offcuts longer than about 6 inches can usually start the next row, so real waste often lands under the 10% allowance.

Sheet vinyl is decided by seam placement rather than by area. Template the room first.

Acclimation and transitions

Wood and laminate need to sit in the room before installation — usually 48 to 72 hours, boxes opened, so the material reaches the humidity it will live in. Skip it and the floor either gaps in winter or buckles in summer, and neither is covered by warranty.

Transitions are the other thing left off most orders. Every doorway between two floor types needs a threshold strip, and every run over about 30 feet needs an expansion gap hidden under trim. Budget for T-moulding, reducers and stair nosing separately — they are priced per linear foot, not per square foot, and they add up faster than expected.

Order it all at once

Flooring is produced in dye lots and batches. Material bought weeks later can arrive a shade off, and on a continuous floor in daylight that difference is permanent and obvious. Order the full quantity, including waste, in a single purchase — and keep the leftovers.
