How Many Sheets of Drywall Do I Need for a Room?

3 August 2026 · 4 min read

Drywall is cheap; hanging it twice is not. The count itself takes two minutes, and the one thing most people get wrong makes them under-order rather than over.

Measure the walls

Wall area is the room's perimeter multiplied by the ceiling height.

Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height

A 12 × 14 ft room with an 8 ft ceiling: 2 × 26 × 8 = 416 sq ft.

If you are boarding the ceiling too, add length × width — another 168 sq ft here, for 584 total. Ceilings are hung first, and they are the reason a drywall lift is the best $40 you will spend on the job.

Do not deduct doors and windows

This is the counterintuitive part. You board straight over an opening and cut it out afterwards. It is faster, the cut line lands exactly where it should, and it avoids putting a seam at the corner of an opening — which is precisely where cracks start.

The offcuts become patches elsewhere in the job. Deducting openings from your estimate leaves you short.

Sheet size changes the seam count

SheetAreaWeight (1/2")
4 × 832 sq ft~52 lb
4 × 1040 sq ft~65 lb
4 × 1248 sq ft~78 lb

Longer sheets mean fewer butt joints — the joints where two square-cut ends meet. They have no tapered edge to hide the tape, which makes them the hardest thing in drywall to finish invisibly. On 12 ft walls, 12 ft sheets eliminate them entirely.

The trade-off is weight. A 4 × 12 sheet is 78 lb of awkward, floppy material. Working alone, 4 × 8 is the realistic choice.

The worked example

12 × 14 ft room, 8 ft ceiling, walls and ceiling

Walls: 416 sq ft  ·  Ceiling: 168 sq ft

Total: 584 sq ft

With 10% waste: 642 sq ft

At 32 sq ft per 4 × 8 sheet

21 sheets

Screws, tape and compound

  • Screws: about 36 per 4 × 8 sheet on 16" centres — every 12 inches along studs, every 8 around a ceiling perimeter. At roughly 320 screws per pound for 1¼" coarse thread, one pound covers nine sheets. Our room needs about 2.5 lb.
  • Joint compound: roughly 1 gallon per 100 sq ft of board for tape plus two coats — about 6 gallons here, so one 4.5-gallon bucket plus a spare.
  • Tape: around 0.4 linear feet per square foot of board, so roughly 235 ft. A 250 ft roll covers it.

These are starting figures. Technique moves compound consumption more than arithmetic does — a heavy hand can double it, and every gram of the excess has to be sanded back off again.

Thickness

1/2 inch is standard for walls and ceilings. 5/8 inch Type X is required for fire ratings, garages and ceilings framed on 24-inch centres, where 1/2 inch will sag between joists over time. 1/4 inch is for skimming over existing walls and curved surfaces.
