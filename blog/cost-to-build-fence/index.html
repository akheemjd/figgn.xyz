How Much Does It Cost to Build a Fence?

3 August 2026 · 5 min read

Fence quotes vary more than almost any other home project. Two contractors can bid the same 100 feet and land $3,000 apart, because the price hides a dozen decisions about material, height, post depth and terrain. Here is what actually drives the number.

The per-foot benchmark

Fencing is priced by the linear foot. For a professionally installed 6 ft wood privacy fence in 2026, most estimates cluster between $20 and $50 per linear foot, with several 2026 data sets putting the typical figure in the $33 to $53 band. Chain link runs cheaper at $10 to $25, vinyl higher at $30 to $60.

On a 100 ft run that is roughly $2,000 to $5,000 installed for wood — and the spread is real, not padding.

Materials versus labour

Labour typically accounts for $8 to $23 per foot, often more than half the total. Wood materials alone run $10 to $20 per foot for pressure-treated pine, $20 to $30 for cedar.

Which means DIY roughly halves the cost — the largest saving available on any project covered here. It also means a weekend digging holes, and post holes are genuinely hard work in clay or rocky ground.

Post spacing sets the count

Eight feet on centre is standard for wood and matches stock rail lengths. Six feet is stiffer and worth it in high wind or on slopes. The post count is always one more than the number of sections — a 100 ft run at 8 ft spacing is 13 sections and 14 posts.

That off-by-one is the most common fence miscount there is, and it means one post short on delivery day.

Depth and concrete

Set posts a third of the above-ground height deep, or below the local frost line, whichever is deeper. A 6 ft fence wants at least 2 feet of hole. In cold climates the frost line governs absolutely: a post set above it will heave and lift the run out of level within a couple of winters.

Budget about two 60 lb bags of concrete per post for a 10-inch hole 2 feet deep. Our 14-post run needs 28 bags. Corner and gate posts carry the load of everything attached to them and deserve wider holes and more concrete.

Gates cost more than you think

A gate is not a section of fence with hinges. It needs heavier posts on both sides, set deeper and in more concrete, plus hardware that will survive a few thousand swings. Budget $150 to $600 per gate for materials on a DIY build, considerably more installed, and more again for a double or drive-through gate.

The worked example

100 ft, 6 ft privacy, 8 ft spacing, one gate

14 posts · 39 rails · 219 pickets

28 bags of 60 lb concrete

DIY materials: roughly $1,300–$2,200

Installed: roughly $2,500–$5,000

Plus $150–$600 for the gate

Terrain and access

A flat, clear, accessible yard is the cheapest fence you will ever build. Everything else adds hours: slopes need stepped or racked panels, tree roots turn a ten-minute hole into an hour, and rock can stop an auger entirely and force hand digging.

Access matters too. If a truck cannot get near the line, every post, rail and picket is carried by hand, and that labour appears in the quote whether or not anyone itemises it.

Wood or vinyl over twenty years

Vinyl costs roughly 50% more up front and needs no staining, ever. Wood needs staining every two to three years at a few hundred dollars a time, which over twenty years quietly closes most of the gap.

The honest trade-off is not really cost, though — it is repair. A damaged wood picket is a five-dollar board and ten minutes. A cracked vinyl panel usually means replacing the whole section and hoping the colour still matches after a decade of sun.

Costs people forget

  • Old fence removal and disposal — often quoted separately.
  • Permits — commonly required above 6 ft, and worth checking before you buy 8 ft posts.
  • A survey — cheaper than moving a fence built on a neighbour's land, which does happen.
  • Staining — every 2 to 3 years, a few hundred dollars each time for a 100 ft run.
  • Slopes and rock — both add labour, and rock can add a lot.

Prices swing hard by region and season. Treat every figure here as a starting point for comparison, not a quote, and get at least three local bids on the same written spec.
