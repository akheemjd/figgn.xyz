MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) is a multiple of resting energy expenditure. 1 MET = sitting quietly. A 6 MET activity burns 6× your resting rate. Calories = MET × weight (kg) × duration (hours).

These are estimates from population-average MET values. Individual efficiency, intensity and body composition cause significant variation. For precise tracking, use a heart-rate monitor or indirect calorimetry.

How It's Calculated

Every activity has a MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) value — a multiple of your resting energy expenditure.

Calories = MET × weightkg × hours

Worked example: 180 lb (81.6 kg) person running at 6 mph (MET 9.8) for 30 minutes. Calories = 9.8 × 81.6 × 0.5 = 400 cal. That's 13.3 cal/min or 800 cal/hr.

One pound of fat is roughly 3,500 calories. At 400 cal per session, you'd burn a pound every 8–9 sessions — assuming intake stays the same, which it rarely does.
