Calorie burn is estimated and varies with intensity, fitness level, and individual metabolism. Use these numbers as a guide, not an exact science. For weight loss, combine with a macro calculator.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select an activity: Choose from running, walking, cycling, swimming, weightlifting, yoga, or HIIT.
  2. Enter duration: How many minutes you exercised.
  3. Enter your weight: Heavier bodies burn more calories per minute.
  4. Results update instantly — see total calories, per-minute burn rate, and the MET value used.

How It's Calculated

Calories = MET × Weight(kg) × Duration(hours)

Example: Running (MET 9.8) × 81.6 kg × 0.5 hours = 400 calories

MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) represents the energy cost of an activity relative to resting. 1 MET = energy burned at rest (~1 kcal/kg/hour).

FAQ

How accurate are MET-based calorie estimates?

MET values provide a reasonable estimate for the average person. Individual factors like fitness level, body composition, exercise economy, and true intensity can shift actual calorie burn by 10-20% in either direction. Use as a guideline, not a precision measurement.

Does weight affect calorie burn?

Yes — significantly. A 200 lb person burns roughly 25% more calories than a 150 lb person doing the same activity at the same intensity. The formula multiplies by body weight directly, so heavier individuals get higher calorie estimates.

Do I burn calories after exercise?

Yes — this is called EPOC (Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption). High-intensity exercise like HIIT and heavy weightlifting create a larger afterburn effect than steady-state cardio. This calculator only estimates calories during the activity, not the afterburn.

How many calories should I burn to lose weight?

One pound of fat equals approximately 3,500 calories. A 500-calorie daily deficit (through diet and exercise combined) leads to about 1 lb of weight loss per week. Use this calculator with a macro calculator to plan your deficit.
