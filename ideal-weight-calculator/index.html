How to Use This Calculator

  1. Choose units: Imperial (feet/inches, pounds) or Metric (centimeters, kilograms).
  2. Select sex: Male or female — each formula has different constants for each.
  3. Enter your height: Use feet + inches or centimeters.
  4. Results update instantly — the range from all four formulas and individual formula results appear.

How It's Calculated

All four formulas calculate ideal weight based on height above 5 feet (60 inches):

Robinson (1983): Male: 52 kg + 1.9 kg/in over 5ft | Female: 49 kg + 1.7 kg/in

Miller (1983): Male: 56.2 kg + 1.41 kg/in | Female: 53.1 kg + 1.36 kg/in

Devine (1974): Male: 50 kg + 2.3 kg/in | Female: 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg/in

Hamwi (1964): Male: 48 kg + 2.7 kg/in | Female: 45.5 kg + 2.2 kg/in

FAQ

Which formula is most accurate?

None of these formulas predicts your personal ideal weight perfectly — they're population-level estimates originally developed for drug dosing. Devine is the most widely used clinically. For a truly personalized assessment, consider body composition (body fat %, muscle mass) rather than weight alone.

Why does the calculator show a range?

Different formulas produce different results because they were developed using different population data. The range (lowest to highest across all four formulas) gives you a reasonable target window rather than a single number.

Are these formulas for athletes?

No. These formulas assume average body composition and don't account for higher muscle mass in athletes. If you're muscular, these formulas may underestimate your ideal weight. Combine with a body fat calculator for a better picture.

What are these formulas actually used for?

They were developed for calculating drug dosages in clinical settings, particularly for medications where body weight affects the correct dose. Devine's formula is still commonly used in hospitals today.
