These formulas were developed by clinicians to estimate drug dosing — not to tell you what you should weigh. No formula knows your frame size, muscle mass or body composition, and the range across the four formulas is more informative than any single number.

Ideal body weight formulas are clinical estimates, not medical advice. They cannot account for individual differences in frame size, muscle mass or body composition.

How It's Calculated

These formulas all start from a base weight at 5 feet and add an increment for every inch above that. The formulas differ in the base weight (for a 5-foot person) and the per-inch increment.

All four formulas:

FormulaMale base (5′)Female base (5′)Per inch
Robinson115 lb108 lb4.1 lb
Miller124 lb117 lb3.1 lb
Devine110 lb100 lb5.0 lb
Hamwi106 lb100 lb6.0 lb

Worked example: a 5′10″ man. Robinson gives 115 + 10 × 4.1 = 156 lb. Hamwi gives 106 + 10 × 6.0 = 166 lb. The range is 156–166 lb, and that 10-pound spread matters more than any one formula's output.
