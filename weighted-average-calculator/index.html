How to Use the Weighted Average Calculator

  1. Enter each value in the left column — a score, a price, a rate.
  2. Enter its weight beside it. Percentages, credit hours, quantities and hours all work.
  3. Use Add row for more entries, or the remove button to drop one.
  4. Read the weighted mean. The simple average sits beside it so you can see what the weighting did.

How It's Calculated

A weighted average multiplies every value by its weight, adds those products, and divides by the total weight. A simple average is the special case where every weight is 1.

Weighted average = Σ (value × weight) ÷ Σ weight

Worked example: scores of 90, 85 and 78 weighted 30, 50 and 20 give (90×30) + (85×50) + (78×20) = 2,700 + 4,250 + 1,560 = 8,510 quality points across a total weight of 100, so 85.10. The simple average of the same three scores is 84.33 — the weighting pulls the result toward the 85, because that value carries half the total weight.

Weights do not have to sum to 100, or to anything in particular. Credit hours, item counts, hours worked and dollar amounts are all valid weights, because the division by total weight normalises whatever scale you use.

Rows with a blank or zero weight are ignored entirely rather than counted as zero — a zero-weight item contributes nothing and should not drag the average down.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I use a weighted average instead of a simple one?

Whenever the things you are averaging are not equally important or not equally sized. A final exam worth 40% of a grade should not count the same as a quiz worth 5%, and a 4-credit course should move a GPA more than a 1-credit one. If every item genuinely carries equal importance, a simple average is the correct tool and gives the same answer.

Do the weights have to add up to 100?

No. The formula divides by the total weight, so any consistent scale works — 30/50/20 and 3/5/2 produce identical results. Percentages are just the most familiar case. What matters is that all weights use the same unit.

What happens to rows with zero weight?

They are excluded rather than treated as zero. A row weighted zero contributes nothing to the total and should have no effect on the result — counting it as a zero would incorrectly drag the average down. Leave the weight blank or set it to 0 to park a row without deleting it.
