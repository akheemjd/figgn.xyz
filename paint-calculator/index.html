How to Use the Paint Calculator

  1. Measure each wall along the floor and enter the four lengths in feet. Leave a wall at 0 if the room has fewer than four.
  2. Enter the ceiling height — 8 feet in most homes, 9 or 10 in newer builds.
  3. Set the number of doors and windows. Each door subtracts 20 sq ft and each window 15 sq ft of wall you do not have to paint.
  4. Choose your coats — two is standard. The gallons figure rounds up, because you cannot buy 2.1 cans.

How It's Calculated

Wall area is the perimeter multiplied by the ceiling height, minus the openings you are not painting.

Wall area = (wall₁ + wall₂ + wall₃ + wall₄) × height − (doors × 20) − (windows × 15)
Gallons = ⌈ wall area × coats ÷ 350 ⌉

Worked example: walls of 12, 14, 12 and 14 feet give a 52-foot perimeter. At 8 feet high that is 416 sq ft. Subtract one door (20) and two windows (30) for 366 sq ft of wall. Two coats is 732 sq ft of coverage, and 732 ÷ 350 = 2.09, which rounds up to 3 gallons.

The deductions are standard sizes: an interior door is roughly 20 sq ft (3 ft × 6 ft 8 in) and an average window about 15 sq ft. Large picture windows or double doors are bigger, so add an extra opening or two to compensate.

Rounding up is deliberate. Running out mid-wall means a second trip and a batch that may not match, so the last partial gallon is always bought whole.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy gallons or a 5-gallon bucket?

A bucket holds the same paint as five gallons but usually costs less per gallon, so it pays off once you need four or more. Buckets also sidestep slight colour differences between separately mixed cans, which shows up on large continuous walls. For one or two rooms, individual gallons are easier to carry, store and return unopened.

Do I need to prime first?

Prime bare drywall, new plaster, bare wood, patched repairs, water or smoke stains, and any glossy surface after scuffing it. Prime as well when going from a dark colour to a much lighter one — primer blocks the old colour far cheaper than extra coats of finish paint. Repainting a similar colour over a sound painted wall does not need it, and a paint-and-primer product is fine.

How much does a gallon of paint actually cover?

About 350–400 sq ft on smooth, previously painted drywall for a single coat. This calculator uses 350, the figure most manufacturers print. Porous or textured surfaces drink more — expect 250–300 on new drywall or heavy texture, and as little as 150 on rough masonry.

Does this include the ceiling?

No — wall area only. For a ceiling, multiply the room's length by its width and divide that by 350 per coat, separately. Ceilings are usually done in a dedicated flat ceiling paint rather than the wall colour anyway.
