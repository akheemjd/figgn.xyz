How to Use the Rebar Calculator

  1. Enter the length and width of the slab in feet.
  2. Choose your grid spacing. 16" or 18" covers most residential slabs; 12" is for heavy loads.
  3. Pick a bar size — #3 for patios and sidewalks, #4 for driveways, #5 for footings.
  4. Buy the stick count. The tie intersection figure tells you how much tie wire you need.

How It's Calculated

A reinforcing grid is two sets of bars at right angles, each set inset from the slab edge by the concrete cover — 3 inches where concrete is cast against earth.

Bars in a direction = ⌊ (clear span ÷ spacing) ⌋ + 1
Linear feet = (bars × bar length) both directions + splice laps
Sticks = ⌈ total linear feet ÷ stock length ⌉

Worked example: a 20 × 10 ft slab at 18" spacing with 3" cover has a clear area of 19.5 × 9.5 ft. That is 7 bars running lengthwise at 19.5 ft and 14 bars running widthwise at 9.5 ft, plus lap allowance where a bar exceeds stock length — giving the linear footage, stick count and tie total shown above.

The +1 matters. A 9.5 ft span at 18" spacing needs 7 bars, not 6 — there is a bar at both ends of the run as well as at every interval between them. Dropping it is the most common rebar miscount there is, and it is why a formula of simply length divided by spacing comes up short.

Splice laps are included. Where a run is longer than your stock bar, the two pieces overlap and both lengths count — the calculator adds one lap per splice at the overlap you choose. Rule of thumb is about 40 bar diameters, which is 20 inches for #4, so the 18" default is close to standard and 24" is conservative.

Weight is the linear footage times the bar's weight per foot, useful when a supplier prices by the pound rather than by the stick.

Frequently Asked Questions

What spacing should rebar be?

16" or 18" on centre covers most residential slabs. Go to 12" for heavy vehicle loads, thick footings or poor soil. Wider than 24" stops working as a grid. Your local code or engineer overrides any rule of thumb — and for anything structural, that is who decides.

Do I need rebar or is wire mesh enough?

Welded wire mesh is adequate for light-duty flatwork like a patio or shed pad, and it is cheaper and faster. Rebar is the answer for anything carrying vehicles, any footing, and any slab over about 5 inches thick. Mesh also has a habit of being walked flat into the subgrade during a pour, which makes it useless — rebar on chairs stays where you put it.

How much should bars overlap?

About 40 bar diameters where bars are spliced end to end — 20 inches for #4, 25 inches for #5. Wire the lap in at least two places. This calculator does not add lap length, so if your slab is longer than your stock bars, add one lap per splice to the total.
