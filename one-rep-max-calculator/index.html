How to Use the One-Rep Max Calculator

  1. Enter the weight lifted in pounds or kilograms.
  2. Enter the reps completed — 3 to 8 reps gives the most reliable estimate.
  3. Pick a formula. Epley is the usual default; Brzycki reads lower at higher rep counts.
  4. Use the training percentages — they are what the estimate is actually for.

How It's Calculated

All of these extrapolate from the observed relationship between load and repetitions.

Epley:    1RM = w × (1 + r ÷ 30)
Brzycki:  1RM = w × 36 ÷ (37 − r)
Lombardi: 1RM = w × r^0.10
O'Conner: 1RM = w × (1 + 0.025r)

Worked example: 185 lb for 5 reps. Epley gives 185 × (1 + 5/30) = 216 lb. Brzycki gives 185 × 36 ÷ 32 = 208 lb. An 8-pound spread from the same set.

Epley and Brzycki agree exactly at 10 reps and diverge either side of it — Epley reads higher below 10, Brzycki higher above. Neither is more correct; they are curve fits to different data.

The estimate is only as good as the set. A set of 5 with three reps left in the tank will underestimate your max substantially, and an estimate from a grinding set with degraded form will overestimate it. For programming purposes the percentages matter more than the 1RM itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which 1RM formula is most accurate?

None consistently, and the differences are smaller than the noise from sleep, food and how the day is going. Epley is the most widely used default. What matters more is picking one and staying with it, so your numbers stay comparable over time.

How do I test a true 1RM safely?

Warm up thoroughly with progressively heavier singles, take full rest between attempts, and work up in small jumps near the top. Use safety bars or a competent spotter, and stop for the day when form degrades rather than pushing one more attempt. Most people never need to test — estimates from heavy sets of 3 to 5 are enough to program from.

Why does my estimate keep changing?

Because performance varies day to day. Sleep, food, stress, time of day and how recently you trained the movement all shift what you can lift by several percent. Trend the estimate over weeks rather than treating any single session as the truth.
