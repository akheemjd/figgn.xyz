How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the weight you can lift for multiple reps (e.g., 185 lbs).
  2. Enter the number of reps you can complete with that weight (best if ≤10 reps).
  3. Choose a formula: Epley (most common) or Brzycki (more conservative at higher reps).
  4. Your estimated 1RM and a full percentage breakdown table appear instantly.

How It's Calculated

Epley Formula: 1RM = Weight × (1 + Reps ÷ 30)

Example: 185 lbs × (1 + 5/30) = 215.8 lbs

Brzycki Formula: 1RM = Weight × 36 ÷ (37 − Reps)

Example: 185 × 36 ÷ (37 − 5) = 208.1 lbs

Epley tends to estimate slightly higher. Brzycki is more conservative, especially at higher rep counts. For best accuracy, test with ≤10 reps.

FAQ

Which formula is more accurate?

Both are well-validated. Epley is the most widely used and tends to be slightly more accurate for lower rep ranges (≤5). Brzycki is more conservative at higher reps and may be better if you're testing with 8-10 reps. For the most accurate estimate, use a 3-5 rep set with either formula.

How should I use the percentage table?

The percentage table is your programming cheat sheet. 85-95% for strength/power, 75-85% for hypertrophy, 65-75% for endurance. Most strength programs periodize across these ranges. Use the table to quickly find your working weights without recalculating.

Can I use this for any lift?

Yes — bench press, squat, deadlift, overhead press, rows, and any barbell or dumbbell exercise. The formula works the same way. Just enter the weight and reps for that specific lift. Each lift will have its own 1RM.

Why shouldn't I use more than 10 reps?

Above 10 reps, fatigue and muscular endurance become bigger factors than pure strength, and the formulas become less accurate. For the best estimate, test with a weight you can lift 10 or fewer times.
